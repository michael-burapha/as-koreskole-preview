The Opportunity

Three audiences. One focused strategy.

The English-speaking client segment is growing, underserved, and willing to pay — and they search differently from Danish students. Here's who we're targeting and why each group is valuable.

🌍
Priority 1

Expats & Internationals

Professionals and families who have relocated to Copenhagen — often from the UK, USA, Australia, India, or other EU countries. They need their Danish driving licence and want to do it in English. They are tech-savvy, research online thoroughly, and are very likely to use AI tools like ChatGPT before Google. High intent, strong conversion.

🎓
Priority 2

International Students

Copenhagen attracts large numbers of EU and international students at KU, DTU, CBS, and KEA. Many are 17–25, want their licence while studying, and communicate primarily in English. They share recommendations actively on Facebook groups and WhatsApp. A single happy student drives multiple referrals.

🔄
Priority 3

Licence Exchange Clients

Non-EU residents must convert their foreign licence to a Danish one — including theory test and practical exam. Many do this in English. This is a growing segment as Denmark attracts more international talent. AS Køreskole already offers this service but doesn't market it effectively to English speakers searching online.

"Students already travel from Fyn to take their licence at AS Køreskole — specifically for the English instruction. That word-of-mouth reach shows the appetite is real. Digital marketing can make this happen at scale."
Why English speakers are more valuable
  • Less price-sensitive — willing to pay for quality + language comfort
  • Less competition — very few Copenhagen schools do full-English courses well
  • Higher referral rate — expat communities share recommendations actively
  • Year-round demand — not tied to the Danish school calendar peaks
  • AI search users — exactly the demographic we'll optimise for in Section 3
What we're NOT doing
  • Abandoning the Danish student market (it's still the core)
  • Paid ads — organic and AI search first, ads only if needed later

  • Chasing every keyword — focused on 12 high-value target terms

AI Search Optimisation (AIO)

Your clients are asking AI assistants, not search engines.

AIO optimisation covers the whole site — both Danish and English. Danish students increasingly use AI tools too, particularly the 17–25 age group who have grown up with them. But the biggest untapped opportunity is the English-speaking audience: expats, international students, and licence-exchange clients who rely on AI tools almost exclusively when researching in a language that isn't their own. The Danish side will be properly structured and AI-ready; the English side is where we push hardest, because right now it's invisible and no competitor has claimed that ground properly.

How AI search works — and where AS Køreskole needs to appear
💬

The query

"What's the best driving school in Copenhagen for English speakers?" — typed into ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Google's AI Overview. This is how expats research. They don't browse 10 websites; they ask and act.

🔍

AI reads the web

The AI tool fetches content from your website, Google Business Profile, review platforms, and trusted directories. It synthesises an answer. If your content doesn't explicitly and clearly answer these questions, you're not included.

📋

The answer is generated

The AI recommends 2–3 schools. If AS Køreskole is there, the phone rings. If not — the opportunity goes to a competitor with a better-structured English page. Currently, AS Køreskole is invisible in these results.

✅

What makes you appear

Clear, structured English content that directly answers common questions. Schema markup (FAQ, LocalBusiness, Course). Strong Google review volume. Mentions on expat forums and directories. Consistent NAP across platforms.

Seasonal Strategy

Demand has clear patterns. Let's prepare for them.

The Danish driving school market has predictable seasonal demand cycles. Rather than reacting when enquiries drop, we build content and campaigns in advance — so that AS Køreskole is already visible and compelling when potential students start searching in the slow periods.

☀️

Spring / Early Summer

March – June
Peak demand

Danish students want their licence before summer. New expats arriving for the academic year. 17-year-olds starting courses after birthday. Very competitive — high organic traffic already.

🌊

Summer

July – August
Medium — English peak

Danish students drop off (holidays), but expat and international student demand holds or grows. Summer intensive courses for English speakers are a strong opportunity not yet fully exploited.

🍂

Autumn

September – October
Medium — new arrivals

New academic year starts. Fresh expat arrivals in Copenhagen settling in — not yet thinking about licence but researching. SEO and content planted now pays off in November–January enrolments.

❄️

Winter

November – February
Low season — target

Quietest period industry-wide. Christmas, dark months, post-exam fatigue. Danish student numbers fall sharply. This is where targeted content for English speakers makes the biggest difference to year-round income.

Why English speakers change the seasonal picture

The Danish student market is tightly tied to the school year, exam periods, and summer holidays. The expat and international student market follows a different rhythm — shaped by visa timelines, employment start dates, and CPR registration. A newly arrived expat in November is just as motivated to get their licence as one in April. Targeting this audience specifically in the slow months is the single most effective way to reduce seasonal income volatility.

Why the current website is a ceiling, not a foundation

⚡

Speed

The current site runs on WordPress — a perfectly valid platform, but one that carries significant performance overhead. The new site is built in clean HTML/CSS/JS with no bloated plugins, no heavy page builders, and no unnecessary database calls. Google ranks fast sites. Slow sites cost you students before they even read a word.

🌍

Bilingual by design

The prototype handles Danish and English natively — not as an afterthought page, but as a first-class dual-language experience with proper hreflang, separate content structures, and language-aware CTAs. The current site treats the English page as a copy-paste of the Danish one.

📱

Mobile-first conversion

Over 70% of driving school searches happen on mobile. The prototype is built mobile-first: thumb-friendly CTAs, a sticky call button, course dates visible above the fold, and a sign-up form that doesn't require pinching or zooming. The current site was not built with mobile conversion as the priority.

The honest case for a new site vs. fixing the old one

This is a conversation worth having directly. Reworking an existing website — especially one built by another developer — is almost always more expensive than building a clean new one. Here's why:

  • Understanding someone else's code structure, theme choices, and plugin dependencies takes significant time
  • WordPress customisations often create hidden dependencies — fixing one thing breaks another
  • Performance improvements on WordPress require paid plugins, server changes, and ongoing maintenance
  • Every hour spent decoding the existing site is an hour not spent building something better
  • The new site will outperform a patched version of the old one on every metric that matters: speed, SEO, AIO readiness, and conversion rate

This is not a criticism of the current site — it's the reality of web development economics. Starting clean is the smarter investment when the goal is performance.

How we protect your existing rankings during the move

Switching to a new website does not mean starting from zero in Google. Before a single page goes live on the new site, we carry out a full SEO migration — extracting everything of value from the current site and reimplementing it properly on the new one. Nothing that is currently working gets abandoned.

Migration Step What it protects
Full keyword audit of the current site Every keyword the current site ranks for — Danish and English — is documented before anything is changed. These become the target list for the new site's content and meta structure.
All existing rankings preserved in the new site The new site's page titles, H1s, meta descriptions, and content are built around the keywords already ranking — so Google sees continuity, not a new website with no history.
301 redirects for every changed URL If a page existed at /koerekort-priser/ on the old site and is now at /priser/, a 301 redirect automatically passes all ranking authority from the old URL to the new one. No traffic is lost to a dead link. Every page is accounted for.
Schema and structured data carried over Any schema that exists on the current site is migrated and improved on the new one — so AI search visibility is not disrupted during the transition.
Google Search Console re-verification The new site is submitted to Google Search Console immediately on launch, with a sitemap, so Google begins re-crawling the new structure as fast as possible.
The goal is a migration Google barely notices — and users don't notice at all. Done correctly, a new site launch can actually improve rankings within 4–6 weeks, not damage them.
